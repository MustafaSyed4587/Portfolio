Business Case Builder

Should a small business spend money on a new system? Change the assumptions and watch the net present value, payback, return and risk update. The last chart shows which assumption the decision depends on most.

Scenario (fictional): a 12-person retail shop is deciding whether to replace paper stock counts with inventory-tracking software. Every number here is an invented assumption, meant to show the method.

Assumptions

Five-year cash flow

Bars show net cash in each year (year 0 is the upfront spend). The line is the running total: where it crosses zero is the payback point.

What the decision depends on

Each assumption is moved 20% up and down on its own. Gold bars raise the value, red bars lower it. The longest bar is the assumption to check first.

The method

Net present value discounts each year's cash flow back to today. Payback interpolates where the running total turns positive. ROI is total net benefit over total cost, undiscounted. IRR is the discount rate at which NPV is zero.

What I would add

Benefits that are hard to price, such as fewer stock-outs or happier staff, and a range from each owner instead of one guess per input. The numbers decide less than people think.

What it cannot show

The inputs are made up, so the outputs are an illustration. A real case needs quotes, time studies and sign-off from the people who would use the system.

A practice model with invented numbers. Not financial advice.
