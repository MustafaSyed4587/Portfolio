Project 02 · Security and privacy

Privacy and Security Risk Assessment: Community Food Drive Charity

By Mustafa Syed · Aug 28, 2026 · Hypothetical charity, real method

Summary

Northside Community Food Drive keeps sensitive household data in shared spreadsheets guarded only by passwords, so two risks score High and eight score Medium. Four fixes in the first week remove most of the exposure.

About this case study. Northside is a hypothetical charity invented for this exercise. Its size, tools, and habits are assumptions, listed in the last section. The method is real and applies to any small organization.

The scenario: a volunteer-run food drive with three organizers and about 60 volunteers, some under 18. It collects donations online and in person, and it keeps lists of donors, volunteers, and the households it serves.

The two High risks:

  1. Account takeover. One shared email account and a shared Drive with no second sign-in step. One phished password exposes everything.
  2. Over-shared household list. The list of households served, with addresses and dietary or health notes, is open to anyone with the link and to volunteers who have since left.

Fix first: turn on two-step sign-in everywhere, turn off link sharing, remove people who left, and limit the household list to three named people.

What the charity holds

The Privacy Commissioner of Canada rates a breach by two things: how sensitive the information is and how likely it is to be misused (source). The table ranks Northside's data on that basis. The household list is the most sensitive because it reveals who needs food aid and where they live.

Risk register

Ten risks, ranked by score. Score is likelihood times impact, each rated 1 to 5. A score of 15 or more is High, 8 to 14 is Medium, and below 8 is Low. The ratings are my judgment for this scenario.

Privacy law fit

Canada's federal privacy law, PIPEDA, applies to organizations that carry out commercial activity. The Privacy Commissioner says ordinary charity work such as fundraising, membership fees, and newsletters is not commercial, but selling, bartering, or leasing donor or membership lists is (source). Non-profit status alone does not exempt an organization.

Northside does not sell its lists, so PIPEDA likely does not bind it directly. I still use PIPEDA's ten principles (source) as the benchmark, because donors, funders, and the households served expect that standard. Result: 4 principles partly met, 6 not met.

Principle What it asks Northside today Status
1. Accountability Someone is responsible for personal information No named privacy lead Not met
2. Identifying purposes State why each item is collected Volunteer form gives a reason, household intake does not Partly met
3. Consent Get permission to collect, use, and share Volunteers agree by signing up, households and guardians are not asked Partly met
4. Limiting collection Collect only what is needed Health notes are collected "just in case" Partly met
5. Limiting use, disclosure, retention Restrict use and keep data only as long as needed Lists kept forever and pasted into group chats Not met
6. Accuracy Keep information correct and current Lists updated when someone remembers Partly met
7. Safeguards Protect against unauthorized access Passwords only, link sharing on Not met
8. Openness Explain privacy practices No privacy notice on forms or website Not met
9. Individual access People can see their own information No way to make a request Not met
10. Challenging compliance People can raise a complaint No privacy contact listed Not met

Breach rules. Under the Commissioner's guidance, an organization covered by PIPEDA must report to the Commissioner and notify the individuals when a breach creates a "real risk of significant harm." It must also keep a record of every breach, serious or not, for two years (source). Northside may not be legally required to do this, but a household-list breach would clearly meet that harm threshold, which is why risk R8 matters.

30-day action plan

The plan puts the biggest risk reductions first. Week 1 takes about four hours and covers both High risks. Effort figures are my estimates for a three-person team.

Week Action Effort Risks addressed
1 Turn on two-step sign-in for email, Drive, website admin, and the payment processor 1.5 hours R1, R10
1 Turn off link sharing, share the household list with three named people, remove anyone who left 2 hours R2, R5
2 Set up a password manager and replace the shared email login with named accounts 3 hours R1
2 Enable screen lock, disk encryption, and remote wipe on every device that syncs files 2 hours R3
3 Write a retention schedule, delete lists past it, and remove health notes that are not needed 3 hours R7, R6
3 Add a guardian consent line to volunteer forms and a short privacy notice to forms and the website 2 hours R6
4 Set up a weekly export of key lists to a second location 1 hour R9
4 Write a one-page breach response plan and log template, and run a 30-minute phishing briefing with a call-back rule for payment changes 3 hours R4, R8

Success check at day 30: every account has two-step sign-in, the household list has three named viewers, and the breach log template exists. Re-score the register then. With the fixes in place, R1 and R2 should drop from 20 to about 6 (likelihood 2, impact 3, or lower).

Method, assumptions, and sources

Method. I listed the data the charity holds, named the ways each item could be exposed, scored each risk on likelihood and impact, checked practices against PIPEDA's ten principles, and ordered fixes by risk removed per hour of effort.

Assumptions, all invented for this case study:

  • Three organizers, about 60 volunteers, some under 18.
  • Household, donor, and volunteer lists are kept in shared spreadsheets.
  • One shared email account, with the password known by five people.
  • Donations go through a third-party payment processor, so the charity never holds card numbers.

Limits. This is a desk assessment of a fictional organization, not an audit, and not legal advice. A real assessment would interview people, inspect settings, and confirm which laws apply.
